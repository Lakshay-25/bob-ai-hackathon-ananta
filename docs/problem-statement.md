# Problem Statement

## The Core Challenge
Indian police stations, including major systems like Delhi's ZIPNET, possess vast amounts of location-tagged crime data gathered over years. However, this data is primarily used retrospectively. There is **zero predictive intelligence layer** applied to everyday policing operations at the station level.

## The Operational Reality
Currently, beat officers patrol static, fixed routes regardless of emerging spatial-temporal crime patterns. This means police resources are deployed uniformly, even when specific micro-zones show rapidly escalating risk. 

When a Station House Officer (SHO) plans the week's deployment, they rely on intuition and historical memory rather than data. They lack a tool to anticipate:
1. Where specific crime types may spike next week.
2. How upcoming scheduled events (festivals, political rallies, market days) could shift risk concentrations across their district.
3. How to optimally redeploy limited patrol units for maximum deterrence.

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short
- **Complex Analytics Platforms**: Existing predictive platforms are often too complex, requiring specialized data science training to interpret, making them useless for a busy SHO on the ground.
- **Black-Box AI**: Many AI solutions provide a "risk score" without explaining *why* the score is high, leading to a lack of trust from veteran police officers.
- **Reactive Focus**: Systems focus on logging what *happened* rather than forecasting what *will happen*.

## The Quantifiable Impact
Data-driven patrol redeployment works. Bengaluru's 2023 predictive policing pilot demonstrated that targeted, intelligence-led patrol redeployment can **reduce property crime by 18%** in test zones. The problem is that no accessible, station-level tool exists to bring this capability to the average Indian police station. Drishti solves this gap.
